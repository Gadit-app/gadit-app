## 1. What Gadit is

Gadit (always written in Latin script — never transliterated to גדית / غاديت / Гадит / ガディット)
is a smart multilingual dictionary that ships at **www.gadit.app**.

Type any word in any of **11 languages** (English, Hebrew, Arabic, Russian,
Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Czech, Italian, Japanese) and you get:

- Every meaning of the word — not just the most common one
- 3 real example sentences for each meaning, in context
- Etymology — the actual historical origin of the word
- Idioms and expressions the word appears in
- A kid-friendly explanation
- An AI-generated image of the word
- A "compose your own sentence" practice with smart feedback
- Quizzes, games, and a personal word notebook

Three plans: **Basic** (free), **Clear** (paid, full dictionary + images +
practice), **Deep** (paid, adds quizzes + games + long-term memory).

---

## 2. Where the name comes from — the brand DNA

**"Gadit" comes from a brand-new verb we're putting into the world: "to GAD
something." To GAD a word means to fully get it — all the way through.
Not to skim it, not to "kind of" understand it. To GAD it.**

This is the founder's whole life work in one coined word: *helping people
understand things all the way to the end*. Not skimming. Not "kind of
getting it." You **GAD** it.

Gadit is the place where you GAD a word. Every other dictionary stops at
the surface — a definition, maybe an example. Gadit keeps going until the
word is YOURS.

**The neologism matters.** "GAD" is meant to be repeatable, ownable, a
word the user can say back to us. The same way "to Google" became a verb,
we want "to GAD a word" to feel natural in a sentence:

> *"Wait, I don't really know what 'ephemeral' means."*
> *"Just GAD it."*

When you read the copy you're about to review, this is the feeling we're
trying to land. Every screen, every sentence, every micro-copy line
should pull the reader toward that GAD-it moment.

### Pronunciation and shape of the verb

- **Pronunciation:** "GAD" rhymes with *had / mad / glad*. **NOT** with
  "god."
- **Conjugation we use:**
  - *I GAD it.*
  - *You GAD it.*
  - *She GADs every word she reads.*
  - *I GADed it yesterday.*
  - *I'm GAD-ing it right now.*
  - *Once you GAD a word, it's yours.*
- **In Hebrew copy:** the verb stays in Latin script mid-sentence — same
  rule as the brand name. Example: *"אל תקרא את המילה. **GAD** אותה."*
  We're open to your suggestions for a natural Hebrew form (e.g. לגדית /
  לגדיט / לג'דד), but the default is to keep GAD in Latin so the verb
  remains the same word across all 11 languages.

We don't have to spell the origin out everywhere — but the brand voice
should breathe it. And there should be one or two clear spots on the site
where we name it directly, in a small confident sentence — not a big
"OUR STORY" page. The verb should appear in copy occasionally too, so
the language starts to belong to the user.

## 3. Brand voice — what we want

- **Warm and personal.** Sounds like a real person wrote it, not a SaaS
  template. Direct address — "you," "your." Conversational rhythm.
- **Confident, not corporate.** We trust the reader to be smart. We don't
  explain things they already know. We don't pad.
- **Concrete over poetic.** "Word origin" > "etymological journey." "Save
  this word" > "begin curating your lexical universe."
- **Short sentences when they land harder.** Long sentences when nuance
  matters. Vary the rhythm.
- **A little personality.** A touch of warmth or wit when it fits — never
  forced.
- **Bilingual instinct.** Hebrew copy should feel native-Hebrew (not
  translated-from-English). Same for English. The two are siblings, not
  translations.

## 4. Brand voice — what we want to AVOID

- **AI-flat:** "Gadit offers a comprehensive solution for word
  understanding." Nobody talks like that.
- **SaaS-speak:** "your gateway to understanding," "unlock the power
  of," "elevate your vocabulary," "transform your learning."
- **Buzzword filler:** "AI-powered," "intelligent," "smart," "seamless,"
  "innovative." Either show it or don't claim it.
- **Three-noun lists where all three nouns mean the same thing:** "deep,
  meaningful, profound understanding" — pick one.
- **Apologetic softening:** "we hope you'll find," "we believe that,"
  "we strive to." Just say what's true.
- **Over-promising:** "instantly master any language," "become fluent in
  minutes." Don't.
- **Em-dashes everywhere.** They're a tell. Use them deliberately, not
  as default punctuation. (HE doesn't use the em-dash at all in our copy.)
- **Generic feelings.** "joy of learning," "love of language." Either
  earn it with specifics or skip it.
- **"empower," "journey," "transform," "elevate," "unlock," "embrace."**
  None of these. They're all sins.

## 5. Hard rules (non-negotiable)

- **Brand name "Gadit" is always written in Latin script,** in every
  language. Never גדית, never غاديت, never Гадит, never ガディット.
  Suggest fixes if you spot any transliteration.
- **No mention of Scientology, Applied Scholastics, or "study tech."**
  Gadit is a stand-alone B2C product. The founder is involved in those
  worlds elsewhere — they don't live here.
- **No medical or psychiatric claims** about how Gadit helps with
  attention, learning disabilities, etc. We talk about understanding
  words, not treating conditions.

## 6. Your task

For every line of copy in section 7, ask yourself:

1. Does this sound like a human wrote it, or like a generic SaaS site?
2. Is the GAD-it essence (truly understanding, all the way through)
   audible, or is the copy AI-flat?
3. Could it be 30% shorter without losing anything?
4. Are there buzzwords we should kill?
5. Is anything corporate-soft when it could be confident?
6. Could the verb "GAD" land naturally in this line, or would it feel
   forced? (We want it occasionally, not in every line.)

**Output format — for each line you'd change, give exactly this block:**

```
SURFACE: 7.x section name / key name
LANG: he or en
SEVERITY: low | medium | high
CURRENT: "the existing copy verbatim, in its original language"
SUGGESTED: "your rewrite, in the same language as CURRENT"
WHY: one sentence reason (in Hebrew, since the rest of your response is Hebrew)
```

**Severity scale:**
- **high** — this line actively hurts the brand. Sounds AI-flat,
  corporate, or generic. Replacing it would be a visible win.
- **medium** — works but could be sharper / warmer / shorter.
- **low** — small polish; cite only if you have a clearly better line.

Group your line-by-line suggestions by surface (7.1, 7.2, ... 7.21) so
the output is easy to scan.

**Also flag — at the END of your review:**

- **2-3 places on the site** where the "to GAD it" verb / origin could
  be introduced naturally. One sentence each, not a full About page.
  This is where the user first meets the new word.
- **3-5 places** where the verb "GAD" could appear inside existing copy
  (e.g. inside a CTA, a feature description, a result message) so the
  new verb starts to feel natural through repetition rather than
  explanation.
- Any sections that feel structurally off — i.e. the copy is fine line
  by line, but the section as a whole misses the brand DNA.
- Anything you'd add that isn't there yet.

**Don't rewrite lines that already work.** We're looking for the 20% of
lines that drag down the whole experience, not a full rewrite.
