## Core idea
Build one English cluster that serves South Africa, Australia, England, NZ, Ireland, Singapore and British-curriculum schools in the UAE. One content investment, many markets.

## Facts cited
- **South Africa:** CAPS runs Grade R to 12 and is published openly by DBE. The IEB NSC exams are based on CAPS, so there's no separate IEB curriculum. ISASA has 900+ schools and 210k pupils. IEB 2026: 260+ schools and about 17k candidates.
- **Australia:** Australian Curriculum v9.0, Foundation to Year 10. About 1,240 independent schools with 768k pupils (18.5%); a third of their pupils are culturally and linguistically diverse.
- **England:** 8.9M pupils and 24,499 schools. About 22% don't have English as a first language; EAL at KS4 is 19.5%. Competitors: Bedrock (1,000+ schools and MATs) and Twinkl.
- **New Zealand:** 90,771 ESOL-funded pupils in 1,754 schools (H1 2026), funded at NZ$780 (primary) and NZ$1,000 (secondary) per pupil per year, so schools have a real budget line.
- **Dubai:** 227 private schools, 387k pupils, 185 nationalities. The UK curriculum is the largest: 90 schools, about 144k pupils.
- **Competition elsewhere:** Vocabulary.com has 17k+ lists; Quizlet and IXL are also present.

## Recommendations
- **Positioning:** "Gadit helps every pupil understand the language of every subject", not an EAL tool, which is a positioning trap. Home-language explanation is the wow moment.
- **Against Bedrock:** the teacher teaches the vocabulary of today's lesson to the whole class.
- **Language order:** English (curriculum content), then Arabic (Israel's Arab sector plus the Gulf; explanation language, medium and subject are three separate products), then Afrikaans only after SA is proven, then Dutch and Swedish later.
- **SA language order:** English, then the existing home-language explanations, then Afrikaans, isiZulu and isiXhosa.
- **School order:** independent/private multilingual, then international, bilingual, strong EAL departments, networks, and public schools last.

### South Africa plan
- Start in Gauteng: 50 schools → 15 demos → 10 pilots → 5–7 paying.
- Buyers: head, head of academics, head of learning support.
- Offer: a 60-day "Founding Schools" pilot (CAPS science, maths and social sciences), no integration.
- Channels: founder outreach, warm intros, and ISASA (sponsored webinar "The hidden vocabulary gap across the curriculum", the Weekly Bulletin, SAHISA heads).
- Messaging: "CAPS aligned + IEB ready".

### Other markets
- **Australia:** independent schools in Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane, about 100 hand-picked, reached by LinkedIn and email. Hook: "Pre-teach the exact vocabulary pupils need before every Australian Curriculum lesson".
- **England:** MATs with high EAL. Offer a "Free Curriculum Vocabulary Audit" (e.g. 428 words, 62 groups) instead of "book a demo".
- **Not now:** USA, Germany, France, Spain, India, Brazil.

### Risk rule
Build no full country curriculum before 3–5 design-partner schools. Start with grades 4–8 in maths, science and social sciences, 20–30 topics per subject.
